Three steps

Tick each step as you go. The next one opens by itself.

Step 1 of 3
1Quit Arc completelySo your data copies cleanly

Press ⌘Q in Arc. Closing the window isn't enough. Save anything you're typing first.

Not sure it's closed? Look for the Arc icon in your Dock: a dot under it means it's still running.
2Run one commandNo questions, no password

Open Terminal (⌘Space, type “Terminal”, press Return), paste this and press Return:

curl -fsSL https://arcreborn.cool.computer/install-artbrowser.sh | bash

It takes about 40 seconds and prints each stage (see below). When it says “ArtBrowser is installed”, tick this step.

Want to read it first? Download the installer, open it in a text editor, then run bash ~/Downloads/install-artbrowser.sh --check. That only looks at your Mac and changes nothing.
3Open ArtBrowser and allow KeychainOne-time macOS question

Open ArtBrowser from the Applications folder inside your home folder, or run open ~/Applications/ArtBrowser.app.

macOS asks to use “Arc Safe Storage”. Enter your Mac password (or use Touch ID) and click Always Allow. That unlocks your saved logins and cookies.

You should now see your Spaces, folders and tabs exactly as they were in Arc.

You're on Art.

Your Spaces and tabs came with you. Three things worth knowing:

  • Make it your default browser so links open here: System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Default web browser → ArtBrowser.
  • Keep Arc and its Keychain entry. Don't delete “Arc Safe Storage”. Your saved logins depend on it.
  • It's a one-time copy. Changes in Arc and ArtBrowser don't sync, so use ArtBrowser from now on.

CHECKLooks before it touches anything

Apple Silicon, a supported Arc version, Arc's saved data, Arc closed, enough space, and no links in Arc's data that point outside it. If any check fails it stops with a plain message and changes nothing.

1/5Backs up your Arc data

A copy goes to ~/Library/Application Support/ArtBrowserBackup. It costs almost no extra space, because your Mac shares unchanged data between copies.

2/5Gets the launcher

Downloads one small file (about 1.9 MB, mostly the icon) and checks it against a fingerprint (SHA-256) written into the installer. If it doesn't match, it stops. This is the only thing downloaded, and nothing about you is sent.

3/5Builds ArtBrowser.app

Copies your Arc app, renames it, adds the launcher and the new icon, switches updates off, and signs it on your Mac. Your Arc app is not changed.

4/5Copies your data

Your Spaces, tabs, history, extensions and logins go into ArtBrowser's own data folder. Your Arc data is not changed.

5/5Puts it in place

Moves the finished app to ~/Applications. If anything fails before this, everything it made is removed again and Arc is untouched.

Uninstalling

Quit ArtBrowser, then run curl -fsSL https://arcreborn.cool.computer/install-artbrowser.sh | bash -s -- --uninstall. It asks you to confirm, removes the ArtBrowser app and its data folder, and keeps your Arc, your Arc data and the backup.

Privacy and security

Everything stays on your Mac

There is no account, no server of ours and nothing to sign up for. We never receive your tabs, history, passwords or anything else.

What stays local

  • The installer only downloads itself and one small launcher file. It uploads nothing.
  • Your data is copied from Arc's folder to ArtBrowser's folder, on the same Mac.
  • The backup stays in your own Library folder.
  • ArtBrowser blocks calls to Arc's and The Browser Company's services (account, sync, analytics, feature flags).
  • You can read every line of the installer and the two source files first.

What to be aware of

  • Browsing is normal browsing: the sites you visit see you, as in any browser.
  • The browser engine is frozen and gets no security updates.
  • The app is signed on your Mac, not by Apple, so it lacks the extra macOS protections Arc had.
  • The backup and the copy both hold your data. Treat them like your Arc folder.
  • We tested that it works with Arc's services blocked, but we can't prove every part stays silent. Use a network monitor to check.
Please read: the engine inside is a frozen copy of Chromium 155. Be careful on sites you don't trust, keep macOS up to date, and use a maintained browser for banking and other sensitive accounts. The official route from the Arc team is Dia, which imports your Arc data.
Check it yourself
  • Read the installer: install-artbrowser.sh. It's plain text.
  • Read the two programs we add: Launcher.m and LocalServices.m.
  • Look without changing anything: bash install-artbrowser.sh --check.
  • Watch its network use in any monitor (Little Snitch, or Activity Monitor → Network).
  • Everything is open source on GitHub.
How it's built

A copy of Arc, plus two small pieces

We don't have Arc's source code and we haven't rebuilt Arc. ArtBrowser is your own Arc app, copied and lightly adapted, so it behaves like Arc but doesn't depend on Arc's servers.

WHYWhy does Arc need its servers at all?

Arc signs you in to an account and syncs through Apple's iCloud and Google's Firebase. When the account service disappears, Arc could refuse to start or sign you out.

1The launcher

Starts the copied browser against ArtBrowser's own data folder, refuses to ever use Arc's, and turns updates and sync off.

2The local-services layer

A small library loaded into the app. It supplies your saved local identity so Arc starts without signing in, reports iCloud as “no account”, rejects requests to Arc's and The Browser Company's services before they leave your Mac, and keeps ArtBrowser's own icon in your Dock.

3Signed on your Mac

Changing the app breaks Arc's original signature, so the installer re-signs it on your Mac. That is also why macOS asks about the Keychain once.

TESTHow we checked it keeps working

We launched copies with the clock set past 22 December 2026 and into 2027, with no network, with Arc's app and data made unreadable, and with Arc's servers answering “account disabled” or “account deleted”. They kept starting and restoring Spaces. We could not test a real shutdown, which hasn't happened yet.

The honest list

What works, and what doesn't

Works (we checked)

  • Your Spaces, folders, pinned and open tabs
  • History and bookmarks
  • Browsing, with ArtBrowser's own data
  • A saved Google sign-in, after the Keychain approval
  • It doesn't use the Arc app or Arc's folders

Copied, not fully checked

  • Extensions load, but password managers such as 1Password ask you to unlock them again, and links to their desktop apps aren't verified
  • Saved passwords and other logins
  • Passkeys, DRM video, wallet signing

Turned off

  • Arc account, sync and sharing links
  • Arc Max and other AI features
  • Automatic updates, including browser security fixes
  • Intel Macs (not tested)

Quick answers

Will it still work after Arc shuts down on 22 December?

That's the whole point, and every test we could run says yes. We can't test a real shutdown until it happens, so we can't promise it.

What if I uninstall Arc?

ArtBrowser is a self-contained copy and doesn't use the Arc app or Arc's data folder. Two things to remember: don't delete the “Arc Safe Storage” Keychain entry (some “app cleaner” tools do), and once Arc is gone you can't reinstall ArtBrowser from it, so install ArtBrowser first and keep a copy of Arc.app somewhere.

Do my changes in Arc show up in ArtBrowser?

No. It's a one-time copy. Switch to ArtBrowser and use it from then on.

Links open in Arc instead of ArtBrowser

macOS still has Arc as your default browser. Set it in System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Default web browser → ArtBrowser.
