Install in three steps

  1. Check your Mac and your Arc

    You need an Apple chip (M1 or newer): Apple menu → About This Mac → Chip. Intel Macs aren't supported yet.

    You need Arc 1.170.0 or 1.169.0, opened and signed in at least once. Check it in Arc: Arc menu → About Arc.

    Another version? The installer stops and changes nothing. Update Arc (1.170.0 is the final release) and try again.

  2. Quit Arc, then run one command

    Press ⌘Q in Arc. Closing the window isn't enough. Then open Terminal (⌘Space, type “Terminal”) and paste this:

    curl -fsSL https://arcreborn.cool.computer/install-artbrowser.sh | bash

    It takes about 40 seconds and asks for nothing. When it says “ArtBrowser is installed”, you're done with this step.

  3. Open ArtBrowser

    Find ArtBrowser in the Applications folder inside your home folder, or run open ~/Applications/ArtBrowser.app.

    macOS asks to use “Arc Safe Storage”. Enter your Mac password (or use Touch ID) and click Always Allow. That unlocks your saved logins. Your Spaces and tabs are there, as they were in Arc.

    To open links in it: System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Default web browser → ArtBrowser.

What the installer does

Six things, in this order. If any check fails, it stops and changes nothing.

  1. Checks your Mac: Apple chip, a supported Arc, Arc's saved data, Arc closed, enough disk space.
  2. Backs up your Arc data to ~/Library/Application Support/ArtBrowserBackup. On most Macs this uses almost no extra space.
  3. Gets the launcher: one 1.9 MB file, checked against a fingerprint (SHA-256) written into the installer.
  4. Builds ArtBrowser.app from a copy of your Arc, with updates switched off, and signs it on your Mac.
  5. Copies your data into ArtBrowser's own folder.
  6. Puts it in place in ~/Applications. If anything fails before this, it removes what it made.

Your Arc app and your Arc data are never changed.

If it stops with a message
  • “Arc is still running”: press ⌘Q in Arc and run it again.
  • “Your Arc is version …”: only 1.170.0 and 1.169.0 are supported.
  • “Intel Mac”: not supported yet.
  • “Arc has no saved sign-in”: open Arc, sign in, quit it with ⌘Q, run it again.
  • “Already installed”: remove it first (below), then install again.
  • “Does not match its checksum”: don't use it. Download the installer again.
Uninstall

Quit ArtBrowser, then run curl -fsSL https://arcreborn.cool.computer/install-artbrowser.sh | bash -s -- --uninstall. It asks you to confirm, removes the ArtBrowser app and its data, and keeps your Arc, your Arc data and the backup.

Everything stays on your Mac

There is no account and no server of ours. We never receive your tabs, history, passwords or anything else.

  • The installer downloads two things: itself, and one small launcher file. It uploads nothing.
  • Your data is copied from Arc's folder to ArtBrowser's folder, on the same Mac. Arc's folder isn't changed.
  • ArtBrowser blocks requests to Arc's and The Browser Company's services (account, sync, analytics, feature flags).

What to be aware of

  • The browser engine is a frozen copy of Chromium 155 and gets no security updates. Be careful on sites you don't trust, keep macOS updated, and use a maintained browser for banking.
  • The app is signed on your Mac, not by Apple, so it lacks some of the protections Arc had.
  • The backup and the copy both hold your data. Treat them like your Arc folder.
  • We tested that it works with Arc's services blocked, but we can't prove every part stays silent. A network monitor will show you.

The official route from the Arc team is Dia, which imports your Arc data.

Check it yourself
  • Read the installer: install-artbrowser.sh (plain text).
  • Read the two programs we add: Launcher.m and LocalServices.m.
  • Look without changing anything: bash install-artbrowser.sh --check.
  • Watch its network use in Little Snitch or Activity Monitor → Network.
  • All of it is open source: github.com/mertdeveci5/artbrowser.

How it's built

We don't have Arc's source code and we haven't rebuilt Arc. ArtBrowser is your own Arc app, copied, plus two small pieces of ours, so it behaves like Arc but doesn't need Arc's servers.

Why that's needed. Arc signs you in to an account and syncs through Apple's iCloud and Google's Firebase. When the account service goes away, Arc could refuse to start or sign you out.

The launcher starts the copied browser on its own data folder, refuses to ever use Arc's, and turns updates and sync off.

The local-services layer is a small library loaded into the app. It supplies your saved local identity so Arc starts without signing in, reports iCloud as “no account”, and rejects requests to Arc's and The Browser Company's services before they leave your Mac.

Signed on your Mac. Changing the app breaks Arc's original signature, so the installer signs it again locally. That is why macOS asks about the Keychain once.

How we tested it. We launched copies with the clock set past 22 December 2026 and into 2027, with no network, with Arc's app and data made unreadable, and with simulated server replies saying “account disabled” or “account deleted”. They kept starting and restoring Spaces. Those replies were synthetic: a real shutdown hasn't happened yet, so we can't promise it.

What works, and what doesn't

Works (we checked)

  • Spaces, folders, pinned and open tabs
  • Browsing, on ArtBrowser's own data
  • A saved Google sign-in, after the Keychain approval
  • It doesn't use the Arc app or Arc's folders

Copied, not fully checked

  • History and bookmarks (the data is copied and its databases passed integrity checks)
  • Extensions load, but password managers such as 1Password ask you to unlock them again, and links to their desktop apps aren't verified
  • Saved passwords, passkeys, DRM video, wallet signing

Turned off

  • Arc account, sync and sharing links
  • Arc Max and other AI features
  • Automatic updates, including browser security fixes
  • Intel Macs (not tested)

Questions

Will it still work after Arc shuts down on 22 December?

That's the whole point, and every test we could run says yes. We can't test a real shutdown until it happens, so we can't promise it.

What if I uninstall Arc?

ArtBrowser is a self-contained copy and doesn't use the Arc app or Arc's data folder. Don't delete the “Arc Safe Storage” Keychain entry (some “app cleaner” tools do), and once Arc is gone you can't reinstall ArtBrowser from it, so install ArtBrowser first and keep a copy of Arc.app somewhere.

Do my changes in Arc show up in ArtBrowser?

No. It's a one-time copy. Use ArtBrowser from then on.

Links open in Arc instead of ArtBrowser

macOS still has Arc as your default browser. Set it in System Settings → Desktop & Dock → Default web browser → ArtBrowser.
